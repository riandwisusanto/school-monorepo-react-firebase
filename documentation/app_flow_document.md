# App Flow Document for School Management Application

## Onboarding and Sign-In/Sign-Up
A new user first arrives at the application landing page, which shows an overview of the school management features and invites them to create an account or log in. From this page, the user can choose to sign up with an email address and password. They click the sign-up link, enter their name, email, and a new password, and then submit the form. A confirmation email is sent and, once they verify their address, they can return to the app and use their new credentials to sign in.  

The sign-in page allows returning users to log in with their email and password combination. If they forget their password, they click the “Forgot Password” link, enter their email address, and receive a reset link. After clicking the link in the email, they choose a new password and gain access again. Once logged in, users have access to their personal dashboard. At any time, they can sign out by clicking the profile icon in the header and selecting “Sign Out.”

## Main Dashboard or Home Page
After a successful login, the application checks the user’s role and directs them to the appropriate dashboard. Teachers see a Teacher Dashboard while administrators or TU users see an Admin Dashboard. Both dashboards share a consistent layout with a top header showing the application name, the user’s profile picture, and a navigation menu. A sidebar on the left contains navigation links for Attendance, Journals, and Settings. The main content area fills most of the screen and displays role-specific widgets.

On the Teacher Dashboard, the user is greeted with a summary of today’s schedule and quick action buttons to start an attendance session or add a new journal entry. Below the header, a card shows the list of classes assigned for the day. On the Admin Dashboard, the user sees overview cards displaying total teachers, students, and upcoming schedules. Beneath those cards, a data table lists recent administrative activities, and buttons allow the admin to manage teachers, students, classes, and schedules.

## Detailed Feature Flows and Page Transitions
When a teacher chooses to take attendance, they click the Attendance link in the sidebar. This opens the Attendance Schedule page, which lists their classes for the selected date. The teacher clicks on a specific class, and the app transitions to the Camera View page. Here, a live camera feed appears, and the teacher clicks “Capture Photo” to take a picture. Once captured, the photo is uploaded to Firebase Storage. When the upload completes, the page automatically advances to the Student Attendance Form.

The Student Attendance Form displays a list of students retrieved for that class. Each student’s name appears with a dropdown to select their attendance status, such as present or absent. The teacher makes selections for each student and then clicks “Submit Attendance.” The app writes the attendance records, along with the timestamp and photo URL, to the Firestore `studentAttendance` collection. After a successful submission, the app shows a confirmation message and returns to the Teacher Dashboard.

For teacher journaling, the teacher clicks the Journals link in the sidebar, which loads a chronological list of past entries. A button labeled “Add Journal Entry” appears at the top of the list. Clicking this button opens the Journal Entry page, featuring a form built with React Hook Form and Zod validation. The teacher enters the theme, classroom activities, and personal reflections. When the form passes validation, clicking “Save Entry” sends the data to the Firestore `teacherJournals` collection. The app then navigates back to the journal list and displays the new entry at the top.

Administrators access the Admin panel by clicking “Manage” in the sidebar and choosing one of the modules: Teachers, Students, Classes, or Schedules. Each module displays a data table powered by `shadcn/ui` and TanStack Query. To add a new record, the admin clicks “Add New,” which opens a modal dialog with a form. They fill in the required details for the chosen entity and click “Save.” The new record is written to Firestore, and the table refreshes automatically. Editing or deleting an existing record follows the same pattern: the admin clicks an edit or delete icon, confirms the action in a modal, and the app updates Firestore accordingly before reloading the table data.

## Settings and Account Management
Users access their personal settings by clicking the profile icon in the header and selecting “Settings.” This opens the Account Settings page, where they can update their display name, email address, and password. The email and password changes are handled through Firebase Authentication, while other profile preferences are stored in the user’s Firestore document. A section for notification preferences allows users to opt in or out of email reminders for attendance submissions or journal prompts.

After making changes, the user clicks “Save Settings.” The app validates the input, applies updates to Firebase Auth or Firestore as needed, and displays a success message. Navigating away from the Settings page, either by clicking the sidebar links or using the browser back button, returns the user to their respective dashboard without losing any unsaved progress.

## Error States and Alternate Paths
If a user enters incorrect login credentials, the sign-in form displays an inline error message prompting them to check their email and password. During network interruptions, loading spinners appear on data-driven pages, and if the connection fails, the page shows a full-screen message indicating that the app cannot reach the server. Users can retry the request or navigate back to a safe page.

When a teacher attempts to access an admin-only route, a route guard instantly redirects them to a 403 error page explaining that they do not have the necessary permissions. From there, they can click “Return to Home” to go back to their Teacher Dashboard. Form submissions that fail server validation, such as missing required fields or invalid data formats, display inline error messages next to each affected field. Once the user corrects the data, they may resubmit the form.

## Conclusion and Overall App Journey
From the moment a new user arrives on the landing page to the daily tasks of taking attendance and writing journal entries, the application guides school staff through a clear, step-by-step flow. Account creation and password recovery ensure reliable access, while role-based dashboards keep teachers and administrators focused on their unique tasks. Detailed feature flows support the core goals of recording student attendance, capturing teacher reflections, and maintaining school data. Settings and error handling ensure that users can manage their profiles and recover smoothly from any issues. Altogether, this journey enables teachers and administrators to complete their work efficiently and accurately in a unified, easy-to-use application.
